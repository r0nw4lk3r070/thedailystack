About

European policymakers and open source project leaders meet under Chatham House Rule at the inaugural Open Source Policy & Ecosystem Forum — a one-day, single-track event in Brussels where the rules shaping what you build and ship get debated by the people writing them. If you work with open source in the Leuven area — and most of us do — this is a rare chance to hear directly how the Cyber Resilience Act, the AI Act, and upcoming EU digital sovereignty frameworks will affect your stack, your compliance burden, and your licensing choices.

This is the first edition of the forum, organised by Linux Foundation Europe. Their track record includes the Open Source Summit Europe, the EU Open Source Policy Summit, and the European Open Source Security Forum — all single-track, high-trust events featuring EU Commission officials, national digital policy leads, and maintainers of major open source projects. The Chatham House Rule format encourages candid policy discussion you will not get from published proceedings or press releases.

What Linux Foundation Europe Talks Are Usually About

EU Open Source Regulation

Linux Foundation Europe events are where the Cyber Resilience Act, the AI Act, and the Data Act get dissected by the people who helped draft them and the maintainers who will have to comply. Sessions tend to focus on what the text actually requires, what it leaves ambiguous, and how open source projects should adapt their licensing and governance to stay on the right side of Brussels. If you ship software in Europe, this is the regulatory literacy track you did not know you needed.

Digital Sovereignty & the Sovereign Stack

European digital sovereignty is a recurring theme — not as a slogan, but as a technical question about which infrastructure Europe controls and which it rents. Talks often map the gap between political ambition and the actual state of European open source projects in cloud, identity, and networking. Expect concrete discussion of EU-funded alternatives to US-dominated platforms, and honest assessment of where the Sovereign Stack still has holes.

Open Source Governance & Licensing

From SPDX compliance to licence compatibility matrices, Linux Foundation Europe events treat governance as engineering, not bureaucracy. Speakers are typically maintainers of large-scale projects (Kubernetes, Yocto, Zephyr) explaining how they manage contribution agreements, security disclosures, and community policy at scale. The focus is practical: what processes work, which ones fail, and how to keep a project governable as it grows past a thousand contributors.

Security & Supply Chain Integrity

Software supply chain security is a fixture at every recent Linux Foundation Europe event, and for good reason: the CRA mandates SBOMs, vulnerability disclosure windows, and coordinated patching that will reshape how every open source project operates. Talks usually cover Sigstore and SLSA adoption, reproducible builds, and the operational burden of compliance for small and mid-size projects — the projects most Leuven developers actually maintain or depend on.
