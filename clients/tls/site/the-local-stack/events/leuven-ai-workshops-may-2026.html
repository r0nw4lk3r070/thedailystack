Leuven.AI Workshops and Seminars — May 2026

Eight events across KU Leuven — guest lectures, PhD defences, and a conference on IP and AI

Various dates throughout May 2026  ·  KU Leuven campus, Leuven  ·  Free

About

Leuven.AI — KU Leuven’s institute for AI — is running eight events this May, and if you work with data, models, or policy anywhere near Leuven, this is your month. The programme spans Bayesian inference, neurosymbolic learning, legal tech, voice biomarkers, and a full-day conference on intellectual property and AI. Most events are free and open to the public; no membership required. You pick the talks that match your work and show up.

Leuven.AI brings together over 100 professors and hundreds of PhD researchers across four research lines: Machine Learning & Data Science; Language, Speech & Vision; Philosophical, Ethical, Legal & Societal Implications of AI (PhELSI); and Applications of AI. The institute has been running events since it became a formal KU Leuven Institute in January 2020, and its LinkedIn community has grown to over 5,000 followers. Talks typically run 45–60 minutes with Q&A, aimed at researchers and advanced practitioners.

Speakers this month include Maarten Truyens (ClauseBase) on a decade of legal tech, Gertjan Burghouts (TNO) on neurosymbolic embodied AI, Prof. Zhengjun Yue (SLAI) on voice as a healthcare biomarker, and guest lecturers from TU Munich and Edinburgh on Bayesian methods. Three PhD defences round out the programme. Check individual event pages for room details — venues vary across the KU Leuven campus.

Speakers

Maarten Truyens

Founder — ClauseBase

Maarten Truyens founded ClauseBase, a Belgian legal tech company that has been building AI-powered clause libraries and contract drafting tools since the early 2010s. His guest lecture traces the arc from rule-based expert systems to modern large language models, drawing on a decade of shipping legal AI products in the European market.

Talk: From Expert Systems to LLMs: A Decade of Building Legal Tech

Legal Tech LLMs Expert Systems Contract Drafting

Iason Papaioannou

Technical University of Munich

Iason Papaioannou works on Bayesian inverse problems at TU Munich, focusing on structure-preserving sampling methods that respect the geometry of the posterior. His research addresses one of the core bottlenecks in computational science: making MCMC reliable enough for high-stakes engineering decisions.

Talk: Structure-Preserving MCMC for Bayesian Inverse Problems

Bayesian Inference MCMC Inverse Problems Uncertainty Quantification

Aretha Teckentrup

University of Edinburgh

Aretha Teckentrup is a researcher in numerical analysis and computational statistics at the University of Edinburgh, working on kernel methods and Gaussian processes for high-dimensional and structured settings. Her work sits at the intersection of approximation theory and scalable Bayesian computation.

Talk: Kernel methods in high-dimensional and structured settings

Kernel Methods Gaussian Processes High-Dimensional Statistics Numerical Analysis

Gertjan Burghouts

TNO

Gertjan Burghouts is a senior scientist at TNO, the Netherlands Organisation for Applied Scientific Research. His work on neurosymbolic AI combines logical reasoning with perceptual foundation models, aiming to give embodied systems the ability to plan and act from visual input — a line of research with direct relevance to robotics and autonomous systems.

Talk: Bridging Logic, Vision, and Action: Toward Neurosymbolic Learning with Embodied Foundation Models

Neurosymbolic AI Computer Vision Embodied AI Robotics Foundation Models

Prof. Zhengjun Yue

Shenzhen Loop Area Institute (SLAI)

Prof. Zhengjun Yue researches voice and speech technology at SLAI, with a focus on making speech-based diagnostics interpretable and inclusive. Her work addresses a critical gap in healthcare AI: ensuring that voice biomarker models work across languages, accents, and demographics, not just for the populations they were trained on.

Talk: Voice as a Biomarker: Toward Interpretable and Inclusive Speech Technology for Healthcare

Voice Biomarkers Speech Technology Healthcare AI Fairness Interpretability

Pieter Vanmechelen

PhD candidate — KU Leuven

Pieter Vanmechelen is defending his PhD on multilevel Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods applied to structural mechanics. His work develops efficient sampling strategies for models where running a single simulation is expensive — a common bottleneck in civil and mechanical engineering uncertainty quantification.

Talk: Multilevel MCMC Methods with Applications in Structural Mechanics

MCMC Multilevel Methods Structural Mechanics Uncertainty Quantification

Arash Mohammadali Zadeh Fard

PhD candidate — KU Leuven

Arash Mohammadali Zadeh Fard defends his dissertation on temperature-driven parameter and state estimation for multiphysics systems. His research tackles the inverse problem of inferring internal states from sparse, noisy measurements — essential for monitoring industrial processes where direct sensing is impossible.

Talk: Temperature-Driven Parameter and State Estimation

Parameter Estimation State Estimation Multiphysics Inverse Problems

Loren Nuyts

PhD candidate — KU Leuven

Loren Nuyts defends her PhD on intelligent preprocessing for scalable automated machine learning. Her work focuses on making AutoML pipelines computationally efficient by replacing brute-force search with smart data transformations — moving from “try everything” to “try what matters.”

Talk: Intelligent Preprocessing for Scalable AutoML

AutoML Preprocessing Scalability Pipeline Optimisation
