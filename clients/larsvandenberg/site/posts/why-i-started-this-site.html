Why I Started This Site

26 April 2026 4 min read

There is no shortage of commentary on the European Union. Brussels produces it in volume — press releases, position papers, summits that end in conclusions no one reads. The machinery of commentary is well-oiled. What is harder to find is writing that slows down enough to notice what the machinery is actually doing.

This site is an attempt at that slower kind of attention. Not breaking news — there are plenty of outlets for that — but the longer view. The institutional habits that outlast commissioners. The policy decisions whose consequences arrive years after the press cycle has moved on. The assumptions so deeply embedded in EU governance that they no longer look like assumptions at all.

What to Expect

Essays and analysis, mostly. Some will be timely, responding to a summit or a regulation or a court ruling. Others will take a step back and look at patterns — how the EU's institutional design shapes its outcomes, why certain debates repeat, what the persistent failures of coordination reveal about the system itself.

I write about what I know: the internal logic of EU decision-making, the politics of integration, and the quiet tension between national interests and supranational ambition. I try to write clearly about things that are often written about badly.

The EU is easiest to misunderstand when it is treated as a single actor with a single will. It is, and has always been, a negotiation — continuous, uneven, and often opaque.

A Note on Independence

This site has no paywall, no advertising, and no institutional sponsor. I write here because I want a space where the writing comes first — where the only constraint is the quality of the argument, not the demands of a feed or a deadline.

If you find the work valuable, the best thing you can do is share it with someone who might disagree.
